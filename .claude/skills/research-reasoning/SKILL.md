# Research reasoning & deep understanding

Reason from mechanism and evidence, not from surface plausibility. Make the logic explicit.

## Method
1. **Restate the question precisely.** What is actually being asked? What would a complete
   answer contain? Name the type: descriptive, mechanistic, causal, or predictive.
2. **Surface assumptions.** List what must be true for the obvious answer to hold. Mark each
   as established / plausible / unverified. The unverified ones are where the work is.
3. **Reason from first principles / mechanism.** Build the causal chain explicitly:
   what drives what, through which pathway, under what conditions. For TUDOR-type problems,
   reason biologically (lipid metabolism, statin pharmacodynamics, ascertainment) *and*
   statistically (estimand, bias, signal vs artefact) — and check the two agree.
4. **Distinguish causation from correlation.** Ask: confounder? reverse causality? collider/
   selection? mediation? Would the association survive the obvious adjustments?
5. **Seek disconfirmation.** State what observation would *break* the explanation, then look
   for it. A hypothesis that cannot fail explains nothing.
6. **Consider alternatives.** Generate ≥2 competing explanations and weigh them against the
   evidence before committing. Note which prediction would discriminate between them.
7. **Reason about implications.** If true, what else must follow? Do those follow-ons hold?

## Habits
- Quantify where you can ("how large?", "compared to what baseline?", "over what range?").
- Separate *what we know* from *what we infer* from *what we assume*.
- Name the weakest link in your own argument explicitly.
- Prefer "the evidence supports X, with these caveats" over confident overreach.

## Output
The explicit reasoning chain (assumptions → mechanism → evidence → alternatives → conclusion),
the strongest objection to your own conclusion, and what would change your mind. End with a
calibrated confidence statement, not a bare claim.

> Pairs well with `critical-appraisal` (to vet the inputs) and `loop-thinking` (to iterate
> the argument). When the reasoning needs external evidence, hand off to `academic-research`.
